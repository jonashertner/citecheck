## International comparison: TAB

The Text Anonymization Benchmark (Pilán et al., Computational Linguistics 2022; MIT licence) holds 127
English judgments of the European Court of Human Rights in its test split, annotated by several people.
An entity counts as protected only when every one of its mentions is caught. Run with `build/bench_tab.py`.

| Entities | Caught (micro average over annotators) |
|---|---|
| Person names, direct identifiers | 86.0 % (498/579) |
| All direct identifiers (names, application numbers …) | 42.9 % (498/1,161) |
| Person names, indirect identifiers | 89.3 % (2,171/2,432) |
| All indirect identifiers (dates, places, nationalities …) | 18.3 % (2,981/16,287) |

The check follows Swiss practice, not TAB's: application numbers, dates, places and nationalities count as
public, so they are explained by design, and the share for all identifiers is low for that reason. Person
names are the figure that compares like with like. Some applicants' names are public case names that Swiss
courts cite ("Ravnsborg v. Sweden"), so the word list rightly treats them as common; TAB counts them as identifiers.

## Known limits

- **Names that are ordinary words.** Where a surname is also a common word (Frei, Egli, Weiss, Lang, Sommer, Streit),
  the check cannot tell them apart without understanding the sentence. Such names are explained where the
  corpus uses them as words; the table above names the largest. A name written mostly in lower case elsewhere
  (gara, bataille) is shown when it is capitalised mid-sentence; a German noun that is also a surname (Streit,
  Sommer) is explained after an article and shown elsewhere.
- **Possessive forms** of a name that appears nowhere else in the document are found less often (see above).
- **Combinations** of common facts (age, place, occupation) are not judged.
- **Text in images** is not read; embedded files are not checked.
- The figures come from corpus texts (PDF and HTML extractions, words broken at line ends joined again), not
  from Word files. Word files have cleaner paragraphs; the lists should be no longer there.

This is a preview. The figures are what they are; they will be rerun and updated with every change to the
rules or the word list.
